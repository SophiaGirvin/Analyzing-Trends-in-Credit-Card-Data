# Analyzing-Trends-in-Credit-Card-Data
Statistical analysis of credit card data using R to explore relationships between credit limits, credit scores, student status, income, and marital status.

# Credit Card Data Analysis

## Overview

This project analyzes the Credit dataset from *An Introduction to Statistical Learning (ISL)* using R. The goal was to investigate factors that influence credit limits, credit utilization, and credit scores while exploring differences across student and marital status groups.

## Research Questions

* What factors have the greatest influence on an individual's credit limit?
* Do students have higher credit utilization than non-students?
* Do students have different credit scores than non-students?
* Do married individuals have higher credit limits than unmarried individuals?
* Is there a relationship between marital status and credit score?

## Methods

* Multiple Linear Regression
* Correlation Analysis
* Two-Sample t-Tests
* Bootstrap Confidence Intervals
* Chi-Square Test of Independence
* Data Visualization with R

## Key Findings

* Credit rating and income were the strongest predictors of credit limit.
* Students showed significantly higher credit card utilization than non-students.
* No significant difference was found in average credit scores between students and non-students.
* No significant evidence suggested married individuals have higher credit limits than unmarried individuals.
* Marital status was not significantly associated with credit score.

## Tools Used

* R
* tidyverse
* infer
* ggplot2

## Dataset

The Credit dataset is sourced from *An Introduction to Statistical Learning* by James, Witten, Hastie, and Tibshirani.
